2 • Local Factors in Ecosystem Distribution (影響生態系統分佈的局部因素 pp.60–61)

Altitude (Figure 4.12), , &

While global biomes form horizontal latitudinal bands, the fit is not perfect. Local-scale variations are driven by altitude (lower air pressure allows air to expand and cool; windward vs leeward ), soil composition/texture/pH, and drainage (全球生物群系雖大致呈緯度水平分帶，但局部尺度受三大因素修正：海拔高度［氣壓降低空氣膨脹冷卻、迎風坡地形雨與背風坡雨影區］、土壤［氮磷鉀養分、沙/粉沙/黏土質地與pH酸鹼度］以及排水條件).

🫁 Atmospheric Pressure & Tibetan Adaptation (p.60):

As altitude increases, atmospheric pressure decreases, allowing air to expand and cool while reducing oxygen availability. on the Tibetan Plateau in the Himalayas have evolved larger lungs compared to their body size, enabling them to take in larger volumes of air with each breath to cope with low oxygen (課本 p.60 案例：海拔升高導致氣壓下降、空氣膨脹冷卻且氧氣稀薄；喜馬拉雅青藏高原的牦牛演化出相對於體型更大的肺部，每次呼吸可吸入更多空氣以適應低氧環境).

3 • The Importance of Biodiversity Hotspots & Biogeographical Isolation (生物多樣性熱點與華萊士線生物地理隔離 pp.61–62)

Figure 4.13 Global & The

Criteria (p.61): A region must contain at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species AND have less than a third (<33.3%) of its natural vegetation remaining. Today, 36 areas are designated as hotspots—covering just 2.5% of Earth's land area, yet supporting ~25% of the world's human population (2 billion people). Over two-fifths (>40%) of hotspot species are at high risk of extinction according to the , and almost a quarter (25%) are at very high risk from the climate crisis (國際保育組織定義熱點須滿足兩大條件：擁有至少 1,500 種特有植物，且原始自然植被留存少於三分之一；全球共 36 個熱點，僅佔陸地面積 2.5%，卻居住了全球四分之一人口［20億人］).

Case Study B • National Renewable Energy vs. Local Endemic Survival (p.64 國家能源與地方特有種衝突)

🇧🇷 Brazil: Belo Monte Dam (Xingu River 欣古河水壩) &

🗺️ Google Maps — Belo Monte Dam & Xingu River (Pará, Brazil)
📍 1. Geographic Location & Case Foreword (地理位置與案例前言):

Located at 3.1°S, 51.8°W in Pará state, northern Brazil, the Belo Monte Dam impounds the Xingu River—a major clearwater southern tributary of the Amazon River—at the famous Volta Grande ("Big Bend") rapids near Altamira (貝羅蒙特水壩位於巴西北部帕拉州［3.1°S, 51.8°W］，攔截亞馬遜河主要南岸支流「欣古河 Xingu River」的大轉彎急流段).

🔬 2. Case Situation & Value Conflict (案例情況與價值衝突問題):

• National / Global Renewable Energy Value (p.64): Completed in November 2019 (financed by domestic and international sources), the dam generates hydroelectric power that over the next 50 years will save ~1.14 billion barrels of oil equivalent (~9% of Brazil's proven oil reserves).
• Local Ecological & Indigenous Destruction (p.64): The reservoir flooded 400 km² (~0.01% of the Amazon rainforest), disrupted river flow, displaced Indigenous communities (who rely on Xingu fisheries and cultural territories), and severely threatened river fisheries—affecting ~600 native fish species, including dozens of strictly rapids specialists like the (2019年11月完工，50年可減省11.4億桶石油當量［佔巴西石油儲量9%］；但水庫淹沒400 km²雨林［佔亞馬遜0.01%］，迫遷原住民，並衝擊欣古河約600種原生魚類——其中包括斑馬異型魚在內的數十種嚴格急流特有種！).

✍️ Model Exam Response (Level 3 Full 6 Marks 滿分範文):

At the global scale, biomes are distributed in roughly horizontal latitudinal bands governed by temperature and precipitation (Figure 4.10 & 4.11). Near the equator (0°–10°N/S), high mean temperatures (20°–30°C) and heavy rainfall (2,000–3,000+ mm) support tropical rainforests, whereas between 15° and 30°N/S the sinking limb of the Hadley cell creates arid deserts (0–400 mm). At high latitudes (50°–70°N), cold winters (-20°C) and low precipitation (200–750 mm) produce Taiga (11.5% of Earth's land area), while Arctic permafrost restricts trees in the Tundra (150–250 mm) (結合表4.1與圖4.11數據說明全球氣候與哈德來環流如何決定水平緯度分帶).

However, local factors disrupt this horizontal pattern. First, as shown in Figure 4.12, increasing altitude lowers air pressure, causing air to expand and cool—shifting vegetation from valley grassland at sea level to montane sequoia forest at 2,000m and alpine tundra above 3,000m. Furthermore, orographic rainfall makes the windward West slope much wetter (supporting dense coniferous forest) than the leeward East rain-shadow slope (restricted to dry sagebrush scrub). Finally, local soil texture, pH, and drainage create micro-scale variations: sandy soils drain rapidly and aerate roots for grasses/shrubs, whereas poorly drained clay soils become waterlogged, supporting cattails and sedges and forming species-rich ecotones at wetland boundaries (結合圖4.12海拔降溫、迎風坡地形雨vs背風坡雨影區，以及土壤質地與排水過渡帶完整分析局部變異).

✍️ Model Exam Response (Level 3 Full 8 Marks 滿分範文):

Biodiversity hotspots and ecosystem services are disproportionately vital to planetary and human well-being. As Figure 4.13a shows, the world's 36 designated hotspots (each holding at least 1,500 endemic plant species with under a third of natural vegetation remaining) cover just 2.5% of Earth's land surface, yet directly support 2 billion people (25% of the world's population). With the IPCC warning that over two-fifths (>40%) of hotspot species face high extinction risk, losing these regions would cripple four essential ecosystem services: supporting services (primary production and nutrient cycling), regulating services (mangrove flood buffering and ladybird biological control of aphids), cultural services, and provisioning services—such as the 7,000 plant-derived Western medicines worth US $43 billion, exemplified by Madagascar's rosy periwinkle which provides a 99% remission rate for lymphocytic leukaemia (結合圖A的2.5%面積、20億人口、>40%滅絕風險及馬達加斯加長春花99%白血病緩解率論證其全球價值).

Despite this immense value, ecosystems are not equally valued or protected due to stark economic disparities and competing spatial priorities (Graph B). First, wealth differences between the Global North and Global South dictate conservation capacity: Norway, with a GDP per capita exceeding US $87,000 (2023), maintains 48 national parks (banning motorised vehicles) and pledged US $2.8 billion via NICFI to curb tropical deforestation. In contrast, Madagascar is a critical hotspot where 75.2% of the population lives below the poverty line; local people depend directly on farming and forestry for immediate survival, making conservation reliant on external EU and OACPS funding. Second, national governments often prioritise short-term macroeconomic or global renewable energy targets over local ecosystem sustainability. For example, Brazil's Belo Monte Dam on the Xingu River (completed November 2019) will save 1.14 billion barrels of oil equivalent (9% of Brazil's proven oil reserves) over 50 years, yet it flooded 400 km² of Amazon rainforest (0.01%), displaced Indigenous communities, and devastated river fisheries impacting ~600 native fish species (including strictly endemic specialists such as the zebra pleco) (精準對比挪威$87,000 GDP vs 馬達加斯加75.2%貧困率，以及巴西貝羅蒙特水壩減省11.4億桶石油 vs 淹沒400km²與重創約600種魚類［含斑馬異型魚等嚴格特有種］的衝突).

In conclusion, while the ecological value of hotspots is universally high, their economic valuation is skewed by poverty and conflicting development scales—proving that global conservation requires equitable financial transfers (like NICFI and EU partnerships) that compensate local and Indigenous stewards (高分總結：唯有透過跨國資金補償與兼顧原住民生計，才能消弭全球保育價值與地方生存需求之間的落差).
