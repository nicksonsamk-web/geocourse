🎯 Examiner's Data Reading Clues (閱卷員判讀提示):

1. Quote exact figures (AO2): Contrast vertebrates like birds (97.3%) and mammals (94.2%, 6,596 species) against insects (only 21.0% catalogued despite 1.05 million known species).
2. Explain mechanisms (AO1): Body size, taxonomic bias, and habitat accessibility (deep oceans 2.2M est., tropical canopies, or subterranean soil like Macau's ).

📋 Edexcel Mark Scheme Breakdown (6 Marks = 2 AO1 + 2 AO2 + 2 AO3):
• AO2 Data Manipulation (2 marks): Correctly contrasts high completion in vertebrates (Mammals 94.2% / 6,596 species; Birds 97.3%) with huge uncatalogued gaps in invertebrates/insects (1.05M described out of ~5M, leaving 79% unnamed; global gap = 6.54M out of 8.7M).
• AO1 Geographical Understanding (2 marks): Explains taxonomic bias towards large, diurnal, terrestrial megafauna vs microscopic/cryptic soil invertebrates (e.g. subterranean in Macau) and inaccessible deep-sea ecosystems (2.2M ocean species).
• AO3 Uncertainty & Implication (2 marks): At current rates (+13,000 species/year), cataloguing takes ~10 human lifetimes; silent extinctions of unnamed keystone decomposers may undermine before baseline data is recorded.
✍️ Model Exam Response (Level 3 Full Marks):

As shown in Figure 4.1a, there is a stark taxonomic skew between vertebrates and invertebrates. Over 94% of mammals (6,596 species) and 97.3% of birds (11,188 species) have been formally described, whereas only 21.0% of insects (1.05 million out of an estimated 5 million) are catalogued, leaving 75.2% (6.54 million) of the Earth's estimated 8.7 million species unnamed (引用圖表精準數據對比脊椎動物與昆蟲的命名落差).

This variation occurs firstly due to organism size and visibility: large terrestrial mammals and birds are easily observed, whereas soil-dwelling or canopy insects are microscopic and cryptic—such as the 1.1 mm subterranean blind ant Leptanilla macauensis, which remained undiscovered in urban Macau until 2018. Secondly, habitat accessibility limits discovery in the deep ocean (where 2.2 million species live) and dense tropical rainforests (從物種體型、微生境隱蔽性及澳門細蟻案例解釋原因).

Consequently, this creates severe baseline uncertainty: even adding 13,000 new species annually, full cataloguing would require 10 human lifetimes. Many uncatalogued invertebrates drive vital and pollination, meaning species may go extinct unnoticed ("silent extinction"), causing scientists to underestimate biodiversity loss and ecosystem vulnerability (評估對全球生物多樣性基線與無聲滅絕的影響).

✍️ Model Exam Response (Level 3 Full 8 Marks):

Both genetic and species diversity play complementary, indispensable roles in sustaining ecosystem resilience. As Graph A illustrates, genetic diversity provides the raw biological adaptability required for long-term species survival. By the 1980s, habitat fragmentation restricted the Florida panther to a tiny population (~20 individuals), leading to severe inbreeding and health defects. Following the 1995 genetic rescue—where 8 female Texas panthers introduced new alleles—hybrid kittens drove a rapid recovery towards ~120 individuals over 10 years. With global within-species genetic diversity having declined by 6% since the Industrial Revolution (and island endemics like the Tasmanian devil collapsing by >80% from DFTD cancer), low genetic diversity directly undermines an ecosystem's top trophic regulators (結合圖A數據分析基因拯救與工業革命後6%基因流失對物種適應力的影響).

However, genetic health within a single species is insufficient without broader species diversity (Graph B). Ecosystem services such as pollination, water purification, and nutrient cycling (carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus flows) depend on both species richness and evenness. Graph B demonstrates that a balanced wetland (High Richness + High Evenness, such as a Louisiana swamp with American alligators and egrets, or Macau's Cotai Ecological Zone with Kandelia mangroves and fiddler crabs) achieves 92% nutrient cycling efficiency, whereas a community dominated by a single species (Low Evenness) drops to 58%, and a polluted degraded swamp falls to 24% (結合圖B數據比較豐富度與均勻度對路易斯安那及澳門路氹濕地養分循環的貢獻).

In conclusion, while species diversity directly regulates daily ecosystem services and nutrient cycling rates, genetic diversity acts as the long-term insurance policy that allows those constituent species to adapt to environmental shocks (總結兩者在微觀長期適應與中觀日常生態功能上的互補關係).
