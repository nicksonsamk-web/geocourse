Habitat Loss, Pollution & The Paradox

While agriculture, logging, urban development, and degrade habitats, periodic removes dead organic litter and rapidly returns nutrients to the soil.

1. Negative vs. Counter-Intuitive Human Actions (p.57)

• Negative Human Actions (p.57): Clearing land for agriculture, logging, and urban development causes habitat loss & isolation; industrial discharges and degrade water and soil quality (農業開墾、伐木、都市開發與工農業污染會破壞棲地並阻斷物種遷徙).
• 🔥 The Fire Paradox (p.57): Periodic clears dead organic material that chokes smaller plants and rapidly returns nutrients to soil. However, uncontrolled or over-frequent fire destroys healthy species (週期性控制火燒能清除窒息小苗的枯枝並快速歸還養分；但失控或過頻野火則會摧毀健康物種).

📍 1. Geographic Location & Case Foreword (地理位置與案例前言):

This case compares two contrasting climatic regions within Australia (click the two map buttons above to switch views): (1) Kakadu National Park & Arnhem Land near Darwin in the Northern Territory (12°S latitude), a tropical monsoon savanna with distinct wet and dry seasons; and (2) South-Eastern Australia (New South Wales & Victoria, 33°S–37°S latitude, e.g., Blue Mountains), dominated by dense oil-rich temperate forests (本案例對比澳洲兩個不同氣候區：一是位於澳洲最北端達爾文港附近的「卡卡杜國家公園與阿納姆地［南緯12°］」熱帶季風稀樹草原；二是位於澳洲東南部的「新南威爾斯與維多利亞州［南緯33°–37°］」溫帶富含油脂的桉樹森林).

🔬 2. Case Situation & Ecological Uniqueness (案例情況與獨特性問題):

Real-World Proof of Textbook p.57 Fire Paradox: In Northern Australia (Kakadu/Arnhem Land), Indigenous Aboriginal rangers conduct early-dry-season "cool patch burning"—low-intensity that removes choking dead grass litter, recycles ash nutrients into soil, and stimulates seed release without scorching upper tree canopies. ✍️ Model Exam Response (Level 3 Full 6 Marks):

Temperature acts as a primary physiological limiting factor by governing metabolic and enzyme reaction rates. As shown in Graph A, in extremely cold temperatures near 0°C, an organism's metabolic rate slows down, restricting energy production. As temperature rises, enzyme activity speeds up until reaching an optimum around 40°C. However, any warmer than 40°C causes the reaction rate to fall rapidly to zero by 60°C because enzymes denature and the organism's ability to supply energy is exceeded (精確引用圖A的0°C、40°C最佳值與60°C酵素變性機制).

Under long-term climate warming, species with low heat tolerance must migrate to higher latitudes or altitudes. Graph B shows that the American pika in the North American Rocky Mountains has already shifted its minimum elevation upslope by 350m (from 2,500m in the 1950s to 2,850m in the 2020s), with a projected rise to 3,350m by 2080. Because mountain peaks narrow with altitude, this upslope retreat drastically shrinks habitat area, isolates pika populations on "sky islands", and ultimately leads to local extinction when no higher ground remains (結合圖B海拔數據與面積縮小效應分析高山物種滅絕風險).

Physical factors provide the foundational template for biodiversity over geological and ecological timescales. Through plate tectonics, Madagascar split from Gondwana and India around 90 million years ago (Fig 4.8), creating oceanic isolation that produced over 90% endemism via vicariance and subsequent dispersal (such as ring-tailed lemurs arriving from Africa ~54 mya and diversifying into at least 50 endemic species). At a regional scale, larger areas support more ecosystems (wetlands, grasslands, forests), while ecological succession (Graph A) gradually increases community complexity—such as sand dunes developing from pioneer marram grass to scrub. Crucially, however, Graph A shows that late successional climax stages actually experience a drop in biodiversity because a few dominant species outcompete smaller plants (先論證板塊構造、馬達加斯加90%特有種，以及圖A演替晚期因優勢種競爭導致多樣性回落的自然規律).

Nevertheless, human actions and fire management increasingly override these natural patterns in the Anthropocene. Land clearing for agriculture, logging, and urban expansion causes habitat fragmentation, while industrial and agricultural runoff degrades water and soil quality. Yet human intervention is not exclusively negative: as Graph B demonstrates, periodic controlled burning achieves 91% plant richness compared to only 52% under total fire suppression. Controlled fires remove thick layers of dead organic material that otherwise choke small plants—preventing late-stage competitive dominance—and rapidly return nutrients to the soil (as seen in Australian Indigenous cool burning in Kakadu). Conversely, when fires become uncontrolled or too frequent (Graph B: 26%), healthy species are destroyed (結合圖B數據分析人類污染破壞與控制性火燒［91% vs 52% vs 26%］如何打破演替晚期壟斷或造成毀滅).

In conclusion, while physical factors (isolation, area, and climate) govern the evolutionary creation of endemic species over millions of years, human activities and fire management are more important in determining whether those ecosystems survive or collapse in the present day (高分總結：自然物理因素決定長期演化上限，而人類活動與火燒管理則決定當代存續與短期動態平衡).
